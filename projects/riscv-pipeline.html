A pipelined RISC-V core, from single-cycle to forwarding

Four labs, one core: start from a single-cycle RV32I design and make it faster without making it wrong.

The path

  • Lab 1: a single-cycle RV32I core.
  • Lab 2: a 7-stage pipeline (two fetch stages, decode, execute, two memory stages, writeback), first stalling on data hazards, then with full forwarding. This was my part.
  • Lab 3: branch prediction with a branch target buffer and 1- and 2-bit direction counters, flushing on a mispredict.
  • Lab 4: a parameterized set-associative data cache, then tuning the whole design for performance per watt.

Stalling vs. forwarding

A stalling pipeline waits for a result to be written back before a dependent instruction can read it; forwarding passes the result from later stages straight to the instruction that needs it. On the dependency-heavy test, IPC went from 0.377 to 0.991 while the clock stayed within 3% (357 vs. 346 MHz), taking throughput from 135 to 343 million instructions per second.

Where the team ended up

A 2-bit predictor reached about 91% accuracy on two of the three branch tests (68% on the third) and cut cycle counts by 21–30% against always-not-taken. The final core, with a 2-way data cache and rebalanced critical paths, ran at a 2.88 ns clock with a geomean IPC of 0.86 across four benchmarks, about 298 million instructions per second.
