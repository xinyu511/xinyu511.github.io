One network pass instead of thousands of Monte Carlo samples

Randomized smoothing certifies a classifier by asking how its prediction changes under Gaussian noise. The standard answer costs hundreds of forward passes per input. We learned to predict it in one.

The problem

To certify a prediction at noise level σ, you estimate the class distribution of the classifier on many noisy copies of the input. Monte Carlo does this with hundreds to tens of thousands of forward passes for every input and every σ. Our certifier is a network conditioned on σ that maps an image to that class distribution directly.

What I did

  • The data pipeline: train a base classifier, generate offline "vote" datasets by Monte Carlo sampling across a grid of σ values, train the certifier with cross-entropy, KL, or L1 losses, and evaluate with all three.
  • The cost study: for each held-out image and σ, measure the certifier's L1 error against a high-sample reference, then count how many Monte Carlo samples reach the same error and convert both sides to FLOPs.
  • Infrastructure: runs on MNIST, Fashion-MNIST, KMNIST, and CIFAR-10 on lab GPU servers, and a port of training to AWS Trainium with tensor parallelism.

Result

One certifier pass costs 4.1 MFLOPs; one Monte Carlo sample costs 41 MFLOPs. To match the certifier, Monte Carlo needed 160× to 6,400× more compute depending on how accurate the target was, with a median of about 1,300×. The more precise the answer you need, the bigger the gap. One caveat: Monte Carlo was evaluated in steps of 16 samples, so 160× is the smallest ratio the setup can show.
