Measuring who vector search fails

Average recall hides the queries that get bad answers. We measured recall per query and per region of the embedding space, and found the misses are not spread evenly.

The problem

pgvector's HNSW index trades accuracy for speed through its search breadth, ef_search. Benchmarks report mean recall, but recall varies a lot from query to query, and queries from some parts of the embedding space are served much worse than others.

What I built

  • The offline measurement pipeline on top of ann-benchmarks: pgvector HNSW in Docker, swept over index and search settings, recording recall@10 for every query instead of the average.
  • Per-cluster analysis: KMeans over the query vectors, recall distributions per cluster (violin and box plots), and Earth Mover's distance between clusters.
  • New workloads: GloVe, CIFAR-10/100 image embeddings, FairFace, and DBpedia.
  • The runtime side: co-designed the lab's adaptive loop, which assigns each query to a cluster and retunes search breadth per cluster to hold recall up, and helped build its monitoring module.

What the measurements show

At a low search breadth the mean recall is 0.70, but more than half the queries fall below 0.9. Raising the breadth fixes them, at a steep price in throughput:

The misses also cluster. Grouping GloVe-25 queries into ten regions of the embedding space, the worst region's mean recall is 0.16 below the best one's at the same setting. That gap is what a single global search breadth can't fix, and why the lab's system tunes search per region.
