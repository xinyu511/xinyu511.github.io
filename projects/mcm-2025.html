Managing tourism in Juneau with dynamic programming

Juneau, Alaska gets more than a million visitors a year. Our four-day contest paper asked how a city can tax and promote tourism so the economy grows without wearing down its glaciers or its residents.

The model

A tourist-demand model drives three linked models: economic profit, an environmental index that recovers with protection spending and degrades with crowding, and resident satisfaction, which drops when the town is too crowded or taxes are too high. Each year the city picks a tax rate and a promotion budget. Backward dynamic programming chooses those two controls to maximize profit plus weighted environment and satisfaction over five years, subject to a carrying capacity, minimum environment and satisfaction levels, and the city's budget.

The plan it found

The plan keeps visitors roughly flat at 1.5–1.7 million a year and varies the tax between 3.2% and 5.0%. Revenue goes increasingly to environmental protection, so the environmental index recovers steadily while resident satisfaction stays near 0.9. We tested how the plan responds to each input in a sensitivity analysis and adapted the model to Miami, where crowding and visitor behavior differ.
